The first version of the picker asked the model every time, and it was accurate: in our thirty recorded scenarios it put the right clip first twenty-six times. It was also slow enough that people stopped waiting for it. A picker that is right in 800 milliseconds loses to a list that is merely recent in 50, because the person pressing the shortcut already knows what they want and is only looking for confirmation.

The second version inverted the order. The panel opens instantly with the most recent items, and the model's answer arrives a moment later and reorders the list, highlighting its choice. If the person has already pressed enter, the answer is discarded. This felt wrong to build, because it means most of the model's work is thrown away, and it turned out to be exactly right: the hit rate stayed at twenty-six of thirty and the abandonment rate fell to almost nothing.

The lesson we wrote on the wall is that latency is not a metric, it is a feature boundary. Below a certain threshold a feature exists; above it, the feature is a promise the product keeps breaking. Everything we add to the picker from now on has to fit under the threshold or run after the panel is already on screen.
