At 09:41 the queue depth alert fired for the card render workers, and by 09:50 the backlog had grown to roughly four thousand jobs with p95 latency above two minutes. The on-call engineer initially suspected the morning traffic peak, because the previous Monday had shown a similar but smaller bump, and scaled the worker pool from six to twelve instances. That did not help, which was the first real clue: the workers were not saturated on CPU, they were waiting. Tracing a single stuck job showed it blocked inside the measurement step, holding the build lock while a font atlas was being rebuilt from scratch. The atlas cache is keyed on the set of characters a job needs, and a marketing campaign launched that morning had pasted a batch of texts containing a handful of rare CJK characters, each of which missed the cache and triggered a full bake. Because bakes are serialised through one lock, every other job on that worker queued behind them, and the autoscaler, seeing idle CPU, refused to add capacity. We mitigated at 10:22 by widening the cached charset to the full GB2312 level one set plus the characters from the campaign, which brought the hit rate back above ninety-nine percent, and the backlog drained by 10:40. The lasting fixes are three: the measurement cache is now warmed at deploy time rather than on first use, a cache miss emits a metric so we can see the miss rate rather than infer it from latency, and a job whose text contains a character outside the cached set is routed to a separate slow pool so it can never hold up the fast one. We also learned that the autoscaler's signal was wrong for this workload; it now watches queue wait time instead of CPU. Nobody was paged unnecessarily and no user data was affected, but forty minutes of degraded pastes is forty minutes too many, and the campaign team deserves an apology for the timing.
